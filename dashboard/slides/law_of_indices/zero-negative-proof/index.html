JM24 · Proof 3

Zero Exponent: \(a^0 = 1\)

Why is any non-zero number to the power of 0 equal to 1?

Using Quotient Law with equal powers (\(m = n\)):

\(\frac{a^k}{a^k} = 1\)   and   \(\frac{a^k}{a^k} = a^{k-k} = a^0\)

Since both express \(\frac{a^k}{a^k}\), they must be equal!

Therefore: \(a^0 = 1\)   (for \(a \neq 0\))

JM24 · Proof 4

Negative Exponent: \(a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\)

What does a negative exponent mean?

Apply Quotient Law with \(0 - n\):

\(a^{-n} = a^{0-n} = \frac{a^0}{a^n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\)

A negative index represents the reciprocal of the positive power!
