JM24 · Proof 1

Law 1: Product Law

Why does \(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\)?

Definition of exponent: \(a^k = \underbrace{a \times a \times \dots \times a}_{k \text{ factors}}\)

\(a^3 \times a^2 = (a \times a \times a) \times (a \times a) = a^{3+2} = a^5\)

In general: \(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\)

JM24 · Proof 2

Law 2: Quotient Law

Why does \(a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}\) (where \(a \neq 0\))?

Expressing as a fraction:

\(\frac{a^5}{a^2} = \frac{\rlap{/}a \times \rlap{/}a \times a \times a \times a}{\rlap{/}a \times \rlap{/}a} = a \times a \times a = a^{5-2} = a^3\)

In general: \(a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}\)

JM24 · Summary

Product & Quotient Rules

  • Same base multiplication: Add the indices \(\rightarrow a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\)
  • Same base division: Subtract the indices \(\rightarrow a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}\)
