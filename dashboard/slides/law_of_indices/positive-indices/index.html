Laws of Positive Indices

Five rules for multiplying, dividing, and raising powers — proved by counting factors.

\[ a^n = \underbrace{a\times a\times\cdots\times a}_{n\text{ factors}} \]

Click / → to prove each law step by step.

1 Product Law

Why \(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\)

Example: \(a^3 \times a^2\)

\(a\)× \(a\)× \(a\) · \(a\)× \(a\)

Count all factors: \(3 + 2 = 5\)

\[ = \underbrace{a\times a\times a\times a\times a}_{5\text{ factors}} = a^5 = a^{3+2} \]
Law 1:   \(a^m \times a^n = \hl{a^{m+n}}\)
2 Quotient Law

Why \(a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}\)

Example: \(a^5 \div a^2 = \dfrac{a^5}{a^2}\)

\[ \dfrac{a\times a\times a\times a\times a}{a\times a} \]

Cancel two matching factors top & bottom:

\[ = \dfrac{\rlap{/}a \times \rlap{/}a \times a\times a\times a}{\rlap{/}a \times \rlap{/}a} = a\times a\times a = a^{5-2} \]
Law 2:   \(a^m \div a^n = \hl{a^{m-n}} \quad (a\neq 0)\)
2 Extension

When the bottom power is larger

\[ y^5 \div y^{15} \]

★ Note: larger index minus smaller — \(15 - 5 = 10\)

\[ = \dfrac{y^5}{y^{15}} \quad\text{(divide num. & den. by } y^5\text{)} \]
\[ = \dfrac{1}{\hl{y^{10}}} \]
So \(\dfrac{y^5}{y^{15}} = \dfrac{1}{y^{10}}\)
3 Power of a Power

Why \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\)

Example: \((a^3)^2\) means two groups of \(a^3\)

\(a^3\)×\(a^3\)

Expand each group, then use Law 1:

\[ = (a\times a\times a)\times(a\times a\times a) = a^{3+3} = a^{3\times 2} \]
Law 3:   \((a^m)^n = \hl{a^{mn}}\)
4 Power of a Product

Why \((ab)^n = a^n \times b^n\)

Example: \((ab)^3 = (ab)(ab)(ab)\)

\(a\)\(b\) · \(a\)\(b\) · \(a\)\(b\)

Regroup: all \(a\)'s together, all \(b\)'s together

\[ = (a\times a\times a)\times(b\times b\times b) = a^3 \times b^3 \]
Law 4:   \((ab)^n = \hl{a^n \times b^n}\)
5 Power of a Quotient

Why \(\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}\)

Example: \(\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^3 = \dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{a}{b}\cdot\dfrac{a}{b}\)

\[ = \dfrac{a\times a\times a}{b\times b\times b} = \dfrac{a^3}{b^3} \]
Law 5:   \(\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \hl{\dfrac{a^n}{b^n}} \quad (b\neq 0)\)
Summary

All Five Laws

5 \(\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^n = \hl{\dfrac{a^n}{b^n}}\)
