Scientific Notation

Definition: write a number as \(a \times 10^{n}\) where \(1 \le a < 10\).
Rule: move the decimal point to just after the first non-zero digit.

Left moves → positive power. Right moves → negative power.

Move Left → \(10^{+n}\)

Large numbers

Decimal point moves left by \(n\) places \(\Rightarrow \times 10^{n}\)
5 0 0 0 .
\[ 5000 = \hl{5 \times 10^{3}} \]
Move Left → \(10^{+n}\)

Example: \(234560\)

2 3 4 5 6 0 .
\[ 234560 = \hl{2.3456 \times 10^{5}} \]

Same for negatives: keep the sign, convert the magnitude.

\[ -234560 = -2.3456 \times 10^{5} \]
Combine powers

Already has a \(10\) power

\[ 428.9 \times 10^{5} \]

Move decimal left 2 places to get \(1 \le a < 10\):

\[ = 4.289 \times 10^{2} \times 10^{5} \]

Add indices (Law 1 for base 10):

\[ = \hl{4.289 \times 10^{7}} \]
Move Right → \(10^{-n}\)

Small numbers

Decimal point moves right by \(n\) places \(\Rightarrow \times 10^{-n}\)
0 . 0 9 1 2 3
\[ 0.09123 = \hl{9.123 \times 10^{-2}} \]
Move Right → \(10^{-n}\)

Example: \(0.00008\)

0 . 0 0 0 0 8
\[ 0.00008 = \hl{8 \times 10^{-5}} \]
\[ -0.00008 = -8 \times 10^{-5} \]
Combine powers

Already has a negative \(10\) power

\[ 0.097 \times 10^{-2} \]
\[ = 9.7 \times 10^{-2} \times 10^{-2} \]

Add indices: \((-2)+(-2)=-4\)

\[ = \hl{9.7 \times 10^{-4}} \]
Negative numbers

Keep the sign out of the way

Negative → scientific notation

1. Ignore the minus sign first.
2. Convert the magnitude as usual.
3. Put the minus sign back at the end.
\[ -428.9 \times 10^{5} \;\;\longrightarrow\;\; -4.289 \times 10^{7} \]
