Scientific Notation

Write a number as \(a \times 10^{n}\) where \(1 \le a < 10\).

Move the decimal point to just after the first non-zero digit.

Left moves → positive power. Right moves → negative power.

Large numbers — move left

Decimal left by \(n\) places \(\Rightarrow \times 10^{n}\)

5 0 0 0 .
\[ 5000 = 5 \times 10^{3} \]

Example: \(234560\)

2 3 4 5 6 0 .
\[ 234560 = 2.3456 \times 10^{5} \]
\[ -234560 = -2.3456 \times 10^{5} \]

Already has a power of 10

\[ 428.9 \times 10^{5} \]

Move decimal left 2 places so \(1 \le a < 10\):

\[ = 4.289 \times 10^{2} \times 10^{5} \]

Add indices:

\[ = 4.289 \times 10^{7} \]

Small numbers — move right

Decimal right by \(n\) places \(\Rightarrow \times 10^{-n}\)

0 . 0 9 1 2 3
\[ 0.09123 = 9.123 \times 10^{-2} \]

Example: \(0.00008\)

0 . 0 0 0 0 8
\[ 0.00008 = 8 \times 10^{-5} \]
\[ -0.00008 = -8 \times 10^{-5} \]

Combine negative powers

\[ 0.097 \times 10^{-2} \]
\[ = 9.7 \times 10^{-2} \times 10^{-2} \]

Add indices: \((-2)+(-2)=-4\)

\[ = 9.7 \times 10^{-4} \]

Negative numbers

1. Ignore the minus sign first.

2. Convert the magnitude as usual.

3. Put the minus sign back at the end.

\[ -428.9 \times 10^{5} \longrightarrow -4.289 \times 10^{7} \]
