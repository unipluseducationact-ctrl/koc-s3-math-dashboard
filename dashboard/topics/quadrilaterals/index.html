3 · Parallelogram reasons: using vs proving

Write the reason in square brackets after each step. Notice the two lists use different wording.

Given ABCD is a //gram, then …

  • AB = DC and AD = BC [opp. sides of //gram]
  • ∠A = ∠C and ∠B = ∠D [opp. ∠s of //gram]
  • AO = OC and BO = OD (O = intersection of diags.) [diags. of //gram]
  • ∠A + ∠B = 180° [int. ∠s, AD // BC]

To prove ABCD is a //gram, show any one:

  • AB // DC and AD // BC [definition of //gram]
  • AB = DC and AD = BC [opp. sides equal]
  • ∠A = ∠C and ∠B = ∠D [opp. ∠s equal]
  • AB = DC and AB // DC [2 sides equal and //]
  • AO = OC and BO = OD [diags. bisect each other]
For a special parallelogram, quote its property: [property of rhombus], [property of rectangle], [property of square]. For exactly one pair of // sides: [property of trapezium].

5 · Common traps

  • Rhombus diagonals are ⊥ but not equal (unless it is a square).
  • Rectangle diagonals are equal but not ⊥ (unless it is a square).
  • “Diagonals bisect each other” is true for every //gram. “Diagonals cut into four equal parts” needs equal diagonals too, so only rectangles and squares.
  • A square is both a rhombus and a rectangle, so it has every property in the table.
  • A diagonal cuts a //gram into two congruent triangles, each with half the area. Triangles with the same height have areas in the ratio of their bases.
  • Mid-pt. thm. halves the length: joining the mid-points of the three sides of △ABC gives a triangle with half the perimeter of △ABC.
